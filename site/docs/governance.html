Governance

Budgets, approvals, audit — the three mechanisms that make a room full of autonomous bots safe to run.

Budgets

Every bot and every room can carry limits in four currencies. The first one that blows pauses the work — no silent overspend, ever.

LimitMeasured inGood for
Tokensinput + output tokensHard cap on model usage regardless of price changes.
Costprovider-listed currency"Don't spend more than ¥20 on this room."
Turnssettled conversation turnsStopping runaway loops by count.
Wall timeelapsed time"One afternoon, max."

Budget meters are always visible in the workstation (right panel and a persistent warning bar above the composer when a room crosses 80%). When a budget stops a bot, the room says so explicitly — the audit log records the stop with the reason.

The approval gate

Bots can read freely, but every write operation pauses for your decision — file edits, shell commands, anything that changes state. The approval card appears right in the chat timeline.

DecisionEffect
Allow onceThis operation proceeds; the next one asks again.
DenyThe operation is refused; the bot is told and can adjust.
Always allow → ruleCreates a standing rule (e.g. "writes under ~/project/notes/") so routine work stops interrupting you.

Rules are ordered and inspectable — deny rules always win over allow rules, and you can review and edit them per bot or globally from the workstation or the REST API.

Audit trail

Every tool call, every approval decision, every budget stop lands in an append-only JSONL log (~/.codar-bot/audit.jsonl) — and surfaces in the workstation's audit page with filters by bot and event kind. It's the honest answer to "what did my bots actually do while I was away?"

Why append-only? Audit logs that can be edited aren't audit logs. The record grows; it never rewrites. Filter views are just lenses over the same immutable lines.

The posture, in one paragraph

Treat bots like talented contractors: welcome in the room, watched at the toolbox. Default settings lean safe — approvals on, budgets on. As a setup earns your trust, promote routine work to rules and raise the budgets. Trust is granted in increments, not assumed at install.
